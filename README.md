<h2> What is Drama </h2>
<p>
Drama is a performing art where actors use speech, movement, facial expressions, and emotions to tell stories and entertain audiences. It has been a part of human culture for thousands of years and is used in theaters, schools, movies, television, and even everyday storytelling. Drama can be serious, funny, emotional, or exciting, and it allows performers to step into the role of different characters and situations.

In drama, every performance depends on teamwork. Actors, directors, stage crew, costume designers, lighting technicians, and sound crews all work together to create a successful production. Rehearsals are an important part of drama because they help performers memorize lines, improve timing, and build confidence before going on stage.

Drama also helps students and performers develop valuable skills outside of theater. It improves communication, creativity, leadership, and cooperation while helping people become more comfortable speaking in front of others. Performing in front of an audience can strengthen confidence and teach responsibility because each person plays an important role in the production.

There are many different types of drama, including comedy, tragedy, musicals, improvisation, and historical plays. Some dramas are meant to make audiences laugh, while others are designed to tell emotional or meaningful stories. Musicals combine acting with singing and dancing, while improv focuses on creating scenes and dialogue without a script.

Drama is more than just acting on a stage. It is a way for people to express ideas, emotions, and creativity while connecting with audiences through storytelling and performance.
